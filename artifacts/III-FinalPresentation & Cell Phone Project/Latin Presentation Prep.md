I decided to take a closer look at this issue at Latin and I am here to share my observations based on surveys that many of you completed. 



The first survey was given in the 23-24 school year. What I was struck with during this time, was the consistent cell phones presented, while concurrently, the 23-24 handbook was very clear that cell phones were not allowed to be in the classroom without permission. 



Here are some observations from that survey (22 respondents): (Can I ask Karen for. a headcount of the number of US faculty?)



100% of Faculty believe that students are distracted by their cell phones daily

6 in 10 faculty believe smartphones have detrimental effects on students

While the handbook, at the time, stated that cell phones "may be confiscated and brought to the US office if the device was used in an appropriate way", only 50% of faculty said that they felt comfortable confiscating a phone. And 54%, at the time, said they were not familiar with the exact policy on cell phones. 



First study in summary - I was very surprised by this study. I was surprised that there was such a large contingent of faculty who were not in support of a cell phone ban. Some solected quotes: "While I would support a ban, I would think it would only be fair to have the same sort of ban for faculty as a good example", "No, I think it would be an overreaction", "No, I believe everyone is entitled to have a phone with them in case of an emergency or to stay in touch with the world"



The most surprising thing to be was the lack of a support for a ban. But also in a short survey, there wasn't a lot of nuance or time for gradients of the spectrum of policies to be asked about. 



And then, in the next school year, a surpries occurred: The new policy - that all students were to put their phones away in a bin upon arrival to class.
