# Diverse CS Lesson Plan



In order to recognize the contributions of diverse, including neurodiverse, individuals to technology, computing, and society, students will utilize their Scratch programming skills to code interactive biographical narratives about important diverse indivduals in the field. Their stories will also examine their impact today. 



Goals

Students will learn about computer science contributions of diverse individuals

Students will explore the themese of equity, diversity, and includion in technology

SWBAT code a Scratch program detailing a biography and explaining the ongoing importance of that individual



Materials Needed:

Short bios, or fact sheets, of several diverse and inclusive historic indivuals that contributed to CS. For example: Alan Turing, Ada Lovelace, Katherine Johnson)

One Scratch exemplar

Worksheet with instructions





Here’s a single-class lesson plan and assignment to engage students in exploring **diverse figures in computer science** and promoting equity, using **Scratch** to tell their stories.

---

### **Lesson Title**:  
Celebrating Diversity in Computer Science: Stories Told Through Scratch  

### **Grade Level**:  
High School Computer Science (Beginner Level)  



**Lesson Plan**  

#### **1. Introduction: Diversity in Computer Science (10 minutes)**  
   - **Hook**: Ask students:  
     *“Why do you think diversity is important in computer science and technology?”*  
     - Lead a brief discussion on how different perspectives drive innovation and help technology serve all people equitably.  
   - **Introduce Pioneers**:  
     - Present a slideshow or handouts with profiles of diverse figures in computer science.  
     - Highlight their contributions, challenges they overcame, and the broader impact of their work.  

---

#### **2. Assignment Overview: Telling Their Story in Scratch (5 minutes)**  
   - **Objective**:  
     - Students will create an interactive Scratch program that:  
       1. Shares the story of a chosen individual in computer science.  
       2. Explores themes of equity and representation.  
       3. Explains why their contributions remain relevant today.  
   - **Features to Include in the Program**:  
     - Use of variables to track interactive elements (e.g., points for answering questions).  
     - At least one loop (e.g., repeating background animations).  
     - At least one conditional statement (e.g., “If you click here, then the story continues”).  

---

#### **3. Brainstorming and Planning (10 minutes)**  
   - Students choose an individual from the provided list or propose their own.  
   - Provide a worksheet with prompts to guide project planning:  
     - *Who is your individual, and what are their major contributions?*  
     - *What challenges did they face, and how does this connect to equity?*  
     - *What interactive elements will your Scratch program include to tell their story effectively?*  
   - Example ideas:  
     - A timeline of Ada Lovelace’s life with animations and dialogue.  
     - A quiz game about Katherine Johnson’s role at NASA.  
     - An exploration of Timnit Gebru’s work in ethical AI.  

---

#### **4. Hands-On Scratch Programming (25 minutes)**  
   - Students begin creating their Scratch programs based on their plans.  
   - Encourage them to incorporate:  
     - **Costumes and Backdrops**: Illustrating different scenes or events.  
     - **Dialogue**: Using the *Say* blocks to share key facts and reflections.  
     - **Interactive Choices**: Allowing users to navigate through the story.  
   - Circulate to assist with coding challenges and provide feedback.  

---

#### **5. Wrap-Up and Reflection (5 minutes)**  
   - **Exit Ticket**:  
     - Students write a quick reflection:  
       - *What surprised you about your chosen figure?*  
       - *Why do you think their story is important today?*  
   - Preview next class: Sharing and presenting completed Scratch projects.  

---

### **Assessment**  
- Evaluate Scratch projects based on:  
  1. Creativity in storytelling and design.  
  2. Effective use of Scratch programming elements (variables, loops, conditionals).  
  3. Connection to equity and relevance of the chosen figure.  
- Review exit ticket responses for engagement and understanding.  

---

### **Extensions**  
- Students can expand their projects to include multiple pathways or mini-games related to the person’s achievements.  
- Display completed projects on a classroom webpage or during a school event to celebrate diversity in computer science.  

This plan encourages students to connect coding skills with storytelling, while exploring the importance of representation and equity in technology.
